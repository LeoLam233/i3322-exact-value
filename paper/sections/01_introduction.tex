\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The \(I_{3322}\) Bell scenario has three binary measurements for each of two
parties. Determining its quantum supremum is a concrete optimization problem
in which the dimension of the underlying Hilbert spaces is unbounded, even
when each strategy is finite-dimensional. Numerical optimization can suggest
a limiting value, but does not identify that value exactly or establish that
all admissible stationary configurations have been accounted for.

P\'al and V\'ertesi studied high-dimensional strategies and the limiting-value
problem~\cite{PalVertesi2010}. Recent preprints by
Pauwels~\cite{Pauwels2026} and Coladangelo~\cite{Coladangelo2026v1} address the
relation between finite-dimensional strategies and variational descriptions.
Mghirbi's released rational witnesses~\cite{Mghirbi2026data,Mghirbi2026paper}
provide finite objects from which a narrow interval can be checked exactly.
Before the present manuscript, Douglas's v1.0.0 Zenodo record displayed the
same candidate constant and a matching/domain-wall picture
~\cite{Douglas2026Zenodo}. The proof claims accompanying that August release
were withdrawn in the author's subsequent public correction; the numerical
candidate itself was not thereby withdrawn. Douglas's later paper
~\cite{Douglas2026Arxiv} develops distinct attainment and nonattainment
claims. The present manuscript does not claim priority for the earlier
candidate or matching picture, and it does not treat the withdrawn proof as
having established the theorem proved here.

Here the finite witnesses are independently verified, while the specific
Coladangelo premises are stated in Section~\ref{sec:upstream}. P\'al--V\'ertesi
and Pauwels supply historical context and comparison, not premises of the
exact-value theorem proved below. In particular, the theorem is not presented
as a consequence of numerical agreement or of a formalization of a different
statement.

Our target \(\St\), defined in Section~\ref{sec:normalization}, is the
\emph{finite-dimensional, projective tensor-product supremum}. The subscript
\(\mathrm t\) denotes this scope. It is unrelated to the outer-tail variable
\(t=a(s)\) used later. No commuting-operator supremum is identified with
\(\St\), and no such identification is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-value characterization]\label{thm:main}
Let \(\St\) be the supremum in Definition~\ref{def:St}, and let
\[
 L=0.2508753845139765,\qquad U=0.2508753855,\qquad I=(L,U].
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_s\) be the full-state matching set in
Definition~\ref{def:matching}, with valid decreasing chains and crossing
convention \(x\geq0>y\). There is a unique \(s_*\in I\) with
\(\mathcal M_{s_*}\ne\varnothing\), and
\[
 \boxed{\St=s_*.}
\]
Equivalently, with the exactly specified analytic germ, propagated chart,
implicit phase and residual of Section~\ref{sec:uniqueness},
\[
 \St=\sigma(t_*),\qquad
 \Res(t_*)=0,\qquad t_*\in a(I),
\]
where \(t_*\) is the unique zero on that entire interval. The exact rational
endpoints \(\ell,h\) displayed below satisfy \(\ell<\St<h\).
The conclusion uses External premises~\ref{prem:Coladangelo}
and~\ref{prem:stable}, together with the finite certificates explicitly
specified in this manuscript.
\end{theorem}

The exact result is the unique-root characterization, rather than a claim
that either decimal endpoint is the value or that an elementary closed form
has been found.

The proof has three mathematical stages. First, a finite lower witness and
a dimension-independent positive polynomial certificate put \(\St\) in
\(I\). A variational compactness argument then produces one positive,
square-summable, strictly decreasing stationary carrier at \(\St\), with
outer endpoint limits. Second, hyperbolic local dynamics turns the carrier
problem into matching two tails in all three state coordinates. Third, a
validated analytic chart and exhaustive finite covering argument treat the
two independent tail phases. Only after the full matching equations exclude
off-diagonal phases is a central scalar residual used. Its derivative has a
strict sign throughout the full parameter interval. The exact identification
is then a short theorem composition; high precision root isolation follows
after uniqueness is established.

This is a machine-assisted proof with replayable certificates. The numerical
subdivisions certify finitely many interval predicates; analytic arguments
explain why those predicates imply statements about every admissible chain.
The proof is not a proof-assistant formalization. The validation record is
reported separately in Sections~\ref{sec:reproducibility}--\ref{sec:validation}
and Appendix~\ref{app:audits}, including the remaining implementation and
provenance caveats and the absence of external expert human validation and
journal peer review of the new exact-value theorem.
